Cadence estimates major and minor triads and the overall key. It shines on clear pop, folk and piano, and can slip on dense or heavily distorted mixes. Tempo may occasionally land on half or double time. Analysis assumes standard A=440 Hz tuning.

This browser doesn't support the Web Audio API that Cadence needs. Try the latest Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.

How does it find the chords in a browser?

When you drop a song, your browser decodes it to raw audio samples with the Web Audio API. Cadence downmixes it to mono, then slides a window across it and runs a Fast Fourier Transform on each chunk to see which frequencies are present. Every frequency is folded onto one of the twelve notes of the octave, building a chromagram: a picture of how much C, C#, D and so on is sounding at every moment. All of this happens on your machine — there is no server and nothing is uploaded.

To name the chords, each moment of the chromagram is matched against templates for all 24 major and minor triads, and the best fit wins. Raw frame-by-frame guesses flicker, so a Viterbi decoder smooths the sequence, preferring to hold a chord rather than jump around — the same idea speech recognisers use. The overall key comes from correlating the song's average note profile with the Krumhansl-Schmuckle key profiles, and the tempo from an onset-strength envelope run through autocorrelation.

What it is good for

Learning a song by ear, transposing to a friendlier key, jamming along, checking the tempo for a remix, or just being curious about what is going on harmonically in a track you love. Because it is instant and private, you can throw voice memos, practice takes, and half-finished demos at it without uploading anything.

Reading the result

The chord map is the whole song at a glance, each block a chord coloured by its root; click anywhere to jump the player there. The chromagram underneath is the raw evidence — bright bands are the notes ringing out. The big letter is the chord playing right now, and the row beside it is the progression. Hit Copy chord sheet to grab the whole thing as text.

Is my music uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser with the Web Audio API. Your file never touches a server, which is why it is instant and completely private.

How accurate is it?

It is a solid estimate, not a transcription. It does well on clear pop, folk, piano and singer-songwriter material, and can struggle with dense mixes, jazz extensions, or heavy distortion. It reports major and minor triads plus the overall key.

What file types work?

Anything your browser can decode — MP3, WAV, M4A/AAC, FLAC, and OGG all work. There is no length or size limit beyond your device's memory.

Why is the tempo sometimes doubled or halved?

Rhythm is ambiguous: a beat and its half-beat both fit the music, so automatic tempo detection can land an octave off. If it reads 75 and feels fast, it's probably 150.

Does it need internet?

Only to load the page. There is no model download and no API call, so once it's open it works offline.
