This browser can't run Retune

Retune needs the Web Audio API with AudioWorklet and microphone access. Try the latest Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari on a device with a microphone.

Wait — real auto-tune, running in a browser tab?

Yes. There is no server doing the work and nothing gets uploaded. When you press start, the page opens your microphone with the Web Audio API and streams the audio through an AudioWorklet, which is a small piece of DSP code that runs on the browser's real-time audio thread. That worklet does the pitch shifting sample by sample, so the corrected voice comes back with only a few milliseconds of delay. The whole signal path lives inside the tab.

How the pitch correction works

Many times a second, Retune estimates the fundamental frequency of your voice using the YIN algorithm, a well-known and accurate monophonic pitch detector. It converts that frequency to a musical note, snaps it to the nearest note allowed by your chosen key and scale, and works out the ratio between where you are and where you should be. The AudioWorklet then pitch-shifts your live audio by exactly that ratio using a granular overlap-add shifter with two crossfaded grains, which keeps the sound continuous while it stretches or compresses the waveform.

Getting the sound you want

The retune speed is the whole trick. At 0 milliseconds your pitch is yanked to the target instantly and held there, which quantizes your continuous singing into hard steps. That stair-stepping is the robotic, modern auto-tune sound. Turn the speed up and the correction glides in gently, so it fixes flat and sharp notes without anyone noticing it is on. Chromatic scale corrects to the nearest of all twelve notes, while picking an actual key and scale keeps you locked to notes that fit the song.

A note on honesty

This is a real-time effect, not studio-grade offline tuning. It does not preserve formants perfectly on large jumps, so extreme corrections take on the classic robotic character rather than sounding invisible. For small, in-key corrections it sounds clean. If you sing roughly in tune and pick the right key, it does a genuinely nice job.

Is my voice uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser with the Web Audio API. Your microphone audio never leaves your device, there is no account, and nothing is stored.

How do I get the classic hard-tune robot sound?

Set retune speed to 0, strength to 100%, and pick a scale like Minor or Minor Pentatonic. Instant retune with no glide is what produces the stepped, robotic effect.

Why should I use headphones?

If you turn on Monitor to hear the corrected voice through your speakers, that sound will leak back into your microphone and cause feedback and double-tracking. Headphones keep the loop clean.

Can I save what I record?

Yes. Press Record while singing and Retune captures the corrected output. When you stop, a player appears with a download link so you can save the clip.

Does it work on my phone?

It works in most modern mobile browsers that support AudioWorklet and microphone access. Latency is a little higher on phones, and you should still use headphones.
